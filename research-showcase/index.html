Nature · 2025 · First author

Y-chromosome loss is a tumor–immune event.

Our pan-cancer analysis spanned 29 tumor types and a single-cell atlas of 1,030,968 cells from 17 epithelial cancers, revealing coordinated loss of the Y chromosome across cancer and T cells—and its relationship to patient outcome.

Research program

One program.
Three moves.

I begin with a clinically meaningful question, resolve the underlying multicellular system, test causality, and return the answer to biomarkers or therapeutic strategy.

01 / Decode

Resolve the ecosystem.

Map genomic alterations and immune states across patients, tumors, and treatment.

  • Cancer genomics
  • Single-cell & spatial multi-omics
  • Clinical cohorts
02 / Test

Separate cause from correlation.

Prioritize and perturb the regulators that shape immune escape and therapy response.

  • CRISPR & Perturb-seq
  • Regulatory modeling
  • AI & virtual perturbation
03 / Translate

Move toward the patient.

Convert mechanism into biomarkers, targets, rational combinations, and testable clinical hypotheses.

  • Urologic oncology
  • Immunotherapy & CAR-T
  • Precision cancer medicine

About

Clinical questions. Causal biology.

I am a physician-scientist and research bioinformatician at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, working at the intersection of urologic oncology, cancer genomics, tumor immunology, and computational biology.

My clinical anchor is bladder and urothelial cancer. My broader work spans pan-cancer immune biology, T-cell state regulation, AI radiogenomics, and emerging CAR-T systems and perturbational genomics. Across each project, the goal is the same: move from patient-derived observation to mechanism and therapeutic relevance.

01How do tumor genomes reshape local and systemic immunity?
02Which cell states and regulators determine response or resistance?
03How can multi-omics and perturbation reveal actionable vulnerabilities?
